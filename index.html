How Arlington's ADU rules actually work

Posted by Joseph Solomon · August 24, 2026

Scroll to see the ADU take shape

If you drive past a single-family home in Arlington with a small cottage tucked in the backyard, there's a good chance it's an ADU — an accessory dwelling unit. It's one of the more concrete tools the town has for adding housing options, and it's had real momentum here since 2021.

Arlington's Town Meeting adopted its ADU bylaw in spring 2021, allowing property owners to build a small second unit on the same lot as their house — attached, detached, or built into existing space — without the special permit process most additions require. The rule applies to both single-family and two-family lots.

There are limits. An ADU can't be larger than 900 square feet, or half the size of the main house, whichever is smaller. Short-term rentals aren't allowed, and the ADU has to stay under the same ownership as the main house — you can't sell it off separately.

The rules got a little more flexible last year. The state's 2024 Affordable Homes Act requires every Massachusetts city and town to allow ADUs by right, without an owner-occupancy requirement. Arlington's Town Meeting updated the local bylaw in 2025 to match, dropping the old requirement that an owner or relative had to live on the property.

In 2024, the town partnered with the nonprofit Housing Corporation of Arlington to install an all-electric, modular ADU behind a two-family home — the first project of its kind here, funded partly through Arlington's Affordable Housing Trust. About a dozen ADUs have gone up since the 2021 bylaw passed, and interest keeps growing. For a family trying to help a parent age in place, or a young adult get a foothold in town, it's a small unit that solves a real problem.
